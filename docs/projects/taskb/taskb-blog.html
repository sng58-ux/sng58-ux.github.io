Building a Dashboard with NHANES Data

How I built a Quarto dashboard to blood pressure, BMI, and smoking in U.S. adults, and what I found.
Author

Sara Giuffre-Coppersmith

Introduction

For Task B, I built a Quarto dashboard that uses NHANES data to explore how systolic blood pressure relates to a specific patient characteristics: body mass index (BMI), smoking status, age, sex, and race/ethnicity. Here I explain why I built the dashboard, how I built it, the hypotheses I tested, and what I learned along the way.

The Data

The dashboard uses data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), specifically the 2017 to March 2020 pre-pandemic cycle.

I combined four NHANES data sets, all matched on the participant identifier (SEQN):

  • Demographics (P_DEMO): age, sex, and race/ethnicity
  • Body measures (P_BMX): BMI
  • Blood pressure (P_BPXO): up to three systolic readings per person
  • Smoking questionnaire (P_SMQ): smoking history

I used adults aged 21 to 79 who were both interviewed and examined which was 7853 participants. I did not remove people with missing values when building the data, so the dashboard reports how much data is missing for each variable.

Hypotheses and Analysis Plan

I focused on two hypothesis:

  • Hypothesis 1: A higher BMI is associated with a higher systolic blood pressure, even after considering age, sex, and smoking status.
  • Hypothesis 2: Systolic blood pressure changes due to smoking status after considering age, sex, and BMI.

To test Hypothesis 1, I first fit a simple linear regression of systolic blood pressure on BMI, then I fit a second model that also included age, sex, and smoking status. Comparing the two models showed how much of the BMI relationship changes once other factors are taken into account. To test Hypothesis 2, I used the smoking term from the adjusted model.

Building the Dashboard

I decided to built the dashboard in Quarto using R from scratch to challenge myself rather than using a template. It is organized into pages so that each topic is easy to find.

Results

Hypothesis 1 (BMI and blood pressure). In the unadjusted model, each one-unit increase in BMI was associated with 0.090 mmHg systolic blood pressure (p 0.0024). After adjusting for age, sex, and smoking, the estimate was 0.108 mmHg per BMI unit (p <0.001). The association became slighly higher after the adjustment which means the additional factors don’t affect BMI versus blood pressure that much.

Hypothesis 2 (smoking and blood pressure). In the unadjusted model and model including sex, never is the highest response in terms of smoking but when compared with blood pressure, never is the response least responded with. This shows that with a higher systolic blood pressure there is also less of a rate of currently being a or previously being a smoker.

What I Learned

Throughout this process I learned how to create a dashboard in R and work with a website on my own. This task was somewhat challenging for me because I decided to take it upon myself to create the dashboard from scratch. I am glad that I did this because it challenged me in a good way and it was a nice learning process. This project showed how a dashboard can turn a large health data set into something readers can explore for themselves.
